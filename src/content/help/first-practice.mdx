Your first practice sets the tone for everything that follows. The good news: there’s very little for you to do. Choose a session, settle the room, and let the guide take it from there.

## Choose a practice

For a first session, start short. A three-to-five-minute breathing practice from the beginning of a themed series is ideal. It introduces the format without asking too much of students who are new to mindfulness.

## Set the room

A calm space helps, but you don’t need silence or special lighting. Invite students to sit comfortably, lower the volume of the room, and let them know that closing their eyes is welcome but never required.

## After the practice

A short reflection helps the practice stick. You might ask one open question, like “What did you notice?”, or offer the journal prompt that comes with the session. There are no wrong answers, and students are always free to pass.

## Tips for a calm session

<Callout type="tip">
  Restlessness on day one is normal. Keep your own posture relaxed, resist the urge to correct
  fidgeting, and trust that consistency does the work over time.
</Callout>

Once your first session feels comfortable, the next step is turning it into a routine. See [Make mindfulness a daily habit](/daily-habit/).
