Your first practice sets the tone for everything that follows. Opt into the 21 Day Quest, open Day 1, settle the room, and let the guide take it from there.

<Callout type="note">
  The first few practices are a gentle introduction to mindfulness. They give students time to
  settle into the routine before they start building deeper skills.
</Callout>

## Start at Day 1 and keep going in order

Begin with Daily Practice, Day 1, then play the next day’s practice the following day. The Daily Practice runs in sequence for all 180 days of the school year, and each session builds on the one before it, so following the order is what turns each practice into a real skill. We recommend starting with the 21 Day Quest on Day 1; [learn more about the 21 Day Quest](/navigating/#the-21-day-quest).

Going in order also takes the decision off your plate. You never have to spend time trying to pick a practice or judge what your class is ready for. Open the platform, and the next day is waiting for you.

<Callout type="note">
  The Daily Practice is your routine, but it isn’t the only thing available to you. There are other
  series for specific moments, like a transition between subjects or a reset after a lockdown drill.
  See [Additional series and lessons](/navigating/#additional-series-and-lessons) for the full list.
</Callout>

## Set the room

Invite students to sit comfortably, lower the volume of the room, and let them know that closing their eyes is welcome but never required.

## After the practice

A short reflection helps the practice stick. A journal prompt has been provided for you and your class. You can include your class’s reflection in the digital journal on the platform, and it will be saved under **My Journals** in your profile settings. Alternatively, students can keep their own journals in a notebook or on a piece of paper.

Journal prompts are available after most Daily Practices. They’re recommended, but always optional.

## Tips for finding ease in the experience

<Callout type="tip">
  Restlessness and resistance in the first few days are completely normal, and they are not a sign
  the practice isn’t working. You don’t need to manage them. Trust the daily repetition to do the
  work. Settling comes from showing up again tomorrow, not from getting today perfectly still.
</Callout>

Once you establish a routine, the experience will be more comfortable. See [Make mindfulness a daily habit](/daily-habit/).
