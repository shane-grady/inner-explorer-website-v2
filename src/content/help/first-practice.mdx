Your first practice sets the tone for everything that follows. The good news: there’s very little for you to do. Open Day 1, settle the room, and let the guide take it from there.

## Start at Day 1 and keep going in order

Begin with Daily Practice, Day 1, then play the next day’s practice the following day. The Daily Practice runs in sequence for all 180 days of the school year, and each session builds on the one before it, so following the order is what turns a few quiet minutes into a real skill.

Going in order also takes the decision off your plate. You never have to pick a practice or judge what your class is ready for. Open the platform, and the next day is waiting for you.

<Callout type="note">
  The Daily Practice is your routine, but it isn’t the only thing available to you. There are other
  series for specific moments, like a reset between subjects or the hours after a lockdown. See
  [Lessons & library](/navigating/#lessons--library) for the full list.
</Callout>

## Set the room

A calm space helps, but you don’t need silence or special lighting. Invite students to sit comfortably, lower the volume of the room, and let them know that closing their eyes is welcome but never required.

## After the practice

A short reflection helps the practice stick. You might ask one open question, like “What did you notice?”, or use the journal prompt if today’s practice includes one. There are no wrong answers, and students are always free to pass.

Journal prompts come with the longer versions of the practices, and they aren’t part of the Early Learning experience.

## Tips for a calm session

<Callout type="tip">
  Restlessness and resistance on day one are completely normal, and they are not a sign the practice
  isn’t working. You don’t need to manage them. Keep your own posture relaxed, let the fidgeting go
  uncorrected, and trust the daily repetition to do the work. Settling comes from showing up again
  tomorrow, not from getting today perfectly still.
</Callout>

Once your first session feels comfortable, the next step is turning it into a routine. See [Make mindfulness a daily habit](/daily-habit/).
