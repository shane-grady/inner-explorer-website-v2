The benefits of mindfulness come from repetition, not intensity. A few consistent minutes each day will do more than an occasional long session. Here’s how to make it stick.

## Pick a consistent time

Anchor the practice to a moment that already happens every day: the start of first period, the return from lunch, or the transition before a test. When it’s tied to an existing routine, you’ll rarely have to remember it.

## Build a simple routine

Keep it the same every day: settle, press play, take a quiet breath at the end. Predictability is part of what makes the practice calming. Students relax faster when they know exactly what to expect.

## Follow the Daily Practice in order

The Daily Practice is designed to be followed in order, building skills gradually over days and weeks. Letting it choose the day’s practice removes the daily decision of what to play, which is one less thing to manage.

<Callout type="good">
  If you miss a day, just pick up where you left off. Inner Explorer saves your place in every

## Keep it going

Milestones along the way mark your class’s progress and give students a quiet sense of accomplishment. Notice them out loud. A simple “that’s two weeks of practice together” reinforces the habit better than any reward.
